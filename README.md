# Mapping and Predicting Flash Floods in Egypt from Space

**Arab Youth Space Hackathon 2026 · 813 Challenge · Theme 4: Climate-Induced Disasters**

A satellite-based workflow for mapping likely flash-flood extent after a storm and identifying terrain that may be susceptible to flooding in future events. The project combines Sentinel-1 radar, Sentinel-2 optical imagery, terrain and land-cover data, validation procedures, and a machine-learning susceptibility model.

> **Important:** This is a research/prototype workflow, not an operational flood-warning service. Its ML labels are derived from the notebook's own flood detector rather than comprehensive ground-truth observations. The susceptibility score ranks relative risk; it is not a calibrated probability and does not predict the timing or magnitude of a flood.

## Project overview

The notebook has two main parts:

### A. Detect flooding after an event

- Compare satellite observations from before and after a storm.
- Use **Sentinel-2 optical imagery** and the **MNDWI water index** to detect changes consistent with new water.
- Use **Sentinel-1 SAR radar** backscatter changes to identify possible inundation, including when optical observations are limited by clouds.
- Apply terrain, permanent-water, and coastal masks to reduce detections caused by steep terrain, existing water, or coastal sediment.
- Fuse the sensor detections and inspect how each processing step changes the estimated flooded area.
- Produce maps, area summaries, sensitivity ranges, and diagnostic checks.

### B. Estimate flood susceptibility before a future event

- Build pixel-level features from terrain, drainage-related measures, land cover, and pre-storm imagery.
- Train a **gradient-boosted decision-tree classifier** on flood labels generated by the detection workflow.
- Evaluate the model with **spatial cross-validation** using spatial blocks to reduce overly optimistic results from randomly splitting neighbouring pixels.
- Test transfer to a second location and generate a relative susceptibility map.
- Rank candidate road-crossing locations for inspection or preparedness.

The notebook also includes a proposed early-warning design that connects susceptibility mapping with rainfall forecasts. The rainfall-trigger and alert workflow is a proposed system design, not a fully deployed alert service.

## Study cases

The notebook defines several candidate Egyptian flood/storm events and configures a primary case study plus a secondary location for transfer testing. The event dates and bounding boxes are approximate and should be checked against reliable event records and available satellite acquisitions before interpreting the results.

The default configuration in the notebook is:

- **Primary event:** Ras Gharib, Red Sea (2016)
- **Secondary event:** South Sinai / Sharm area (2020; location marked as unverified in the notebook)
- **Nominal pixel resolution:** 20 metres

Results depend on satellite coverage, cloud conditions, image timing, event selection, and the thresholds configured in the notebook.

## Data sources

The notebook queries external geospatial data services at runtime. An internet connection is required to retrieve data.

- **Sentinel-1 RTC** radar imagery and **Sentinel-2 L2A** optical imagery through the Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC catalogue.
- **Digital elevation / terrain data** for slope and relative elevation analysis, as configured in the notebook.
- **ESA WorldCover** for land-cover and permanent-water masking.
- **OpenStreetMap** for road and building exposure analysis and candidate road-crossing locations.
- **Open-Meteo reanalysis** for coarse rainfall context.

Please consult each provider's terms, attribution requirements, and data limitations before redistributing derived products.

1. Upload `egypt_flood_detect_and_predict (1).ipynb` to Google Colab, or open the notebook from your GitHub repository.

## Validation and uncertainty

The notebook includes multiple checks intended to make the results auditable:

- Compare detections from optical and radar sensors.
- Inspect before/after imagery and spatial patterns.
- Track how masks and cleanup operations affect the final area estimate.
- Sweep detection thresholds and report a sensitivity range.
- Support manual labelling of sampled points and calculate classification metrics with confidence intervals.
- Test the method on a second event/location without changing the detection thresholds.
- Evaluate the susceptibility model with spatial cross-validation and a cross-location transfer test.

For validation metrics to be meaningful, the sampled points must be reviewed and labelled against suitable imagery or other evidence. Do not report metrics as ground-truth accuracy unless the labels genuinely represent independent reference data.

## Limitations

- **Satellite timing:** imagery may be acquired days after a storm, after flash-flood water has already drained.
- **False detections:** dry sand, terrain shadows, coastal sediment, and other surface changes can resemble flood signals despite filtering.
- **Urban flooding:** radar interpretation can be difficult around buildings, while optical imagery may be cloud-obscured. Street-level urban inundation is not reliably mapped by this workflow.
- **Limited training labels:** ML labels are generated by the detector and are not comprehensive field-verified ground truth. The current event sample is too limited to support a reliable rainfall-to-flood forecast.
- **Risk score interpretation:** susceptibility scores provide a relative ranking, not the probability, timing, or depth of a future flood.
- **Spatial resolution:** the default 20 m grid can miss narrow channels and small inundated areas.
- **Event metadata:** some event locations and bounding boxes are approximate or explicitly unverified and require independent confirmation.

Use the maps as decision-support and screening outputs, not as a substitute for official warnings, field assessment, or emergency-management procedures.
